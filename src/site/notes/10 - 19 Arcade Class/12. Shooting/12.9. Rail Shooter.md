## History

### A Decade of Dominance (1980s)

As far as I can tell, there seems to have been no stopping [[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.01. Sega\|Sega]] when it came to the first ten years of Rail Shooters. The earliest example I can seem to find is their *Buck Rogers: Planet of Zoom* (1982), whose legacy to the subgenre is presumably obscured due to problems with licensing and rereleases.

With [[90 - 99 Resources/92. People/92.09. Yu Suzuki\|Yu Suzuki]]'s work at the company, though, Rail Shooters would truly blossom. *Space Harrier* (1985) and *After Burner* (1987) would be the decisive model for others to follow. Other notable releases from Sega include the excellent *Galaxy Force* (1988) and *Thunder Blade* (1987), the latter of which seamlessly transitioned between [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/12. Shooting/12.5. V-SHMUP\|V-SHMUP]] and Rail Shooter gameplay.
- Overall, this is a subgenre linked heavily with technological evolution, because lots of these arcade cabinets had the vibrating and weird controls and whatnot

#### Others of Note
Outside of Sega? There's something interesting about *Devastators* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.05. Konami\|Konami]], 1988), where gameplay is on foot but still over-the-shoulder (almost like an early [[30 - 39 Social Class/33. TPS/33.00. TPS Genre\|TPS]]!). *Aqua Jack* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.02. Taito\|Taito]], 1989) is also very competent, limiting a traditionally free range of movement to navigating with a physics-based hovercraft.

### Transition to 3D (1990s)

For many Rail Shooter developers, the first half of the 1990s was still a tricky and experimental time. The seminal *Xevious* franchise briefly came to Rail Shooters in the form of *Solvalou* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.04. Namco\|Namco]], 1991), and while not particularly notable for its gameplay, it used full polygons with a first-person perspective rather than the sprite scaling we have seen before. Games like *Total Eclipse* (Crystal Dynamics, 1994) and *Soulstar* (Core Design, 1994) both made attempts at richer, narrative-driven experiences but were bogged down by gameplay reminiscent of the Laserdisc days.

However, someone out there had the technical ability to challenge Sega's dominance of the subgenre. Argonaut Games, a British developer, had somehow managed to fit a 3D experience on [[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.06. Nintendo\|Nintendo]]'s Game Boy with *X* (1992). While *X* only released in Japan, it set the stage for the Super FX chip and *Star Fox* (1993) for the SNES. Sega wasn't necessarily out for the count, though, as the Saturn released with *Panzer Dragoon* (1995) and *Panzer Dragoon II Zwei* (1996) following soon after. Nintendo fired back with *Star Fox 64* (1997), which is arguably the most well-known game in the entire subgenre.

The period that immediately followed seemed to be the first widespread Renaissance for the subgenre, with competent developers bringing a variety of ideas to the latter half of the 1990s:
- [[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.14. Treasure\|Treasure]], the legendary group who had already found success with other [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/12. Shooting/12. Shooting Genre\|Arcade Shooting]] subgenres, released the masterpiece of *Sin & Punishment* in 2000, though it unfortunately only came out in Japan and at the tail-end of the N64's lifespan
- United Game Artists, a division of Sega, released the synesthetic, trance-inducing *Rez* in 2001
- *Gamera 2000* (Surveyor Corp., 1997), *Omega Boost* (Polyphony Digital, 1999), and *Vanark* (Bit Town, 1999) all showed some promise, too

### Slow Death

Ultimately, I think those two dominant franchises embody the challenges Rail Shooters faced.

Rather than Panzer Dragoon seeing a traditional third sequel, the follow-up to *Zwei* was *Panzer Dragoon Saga* (1998), an [[20 - 29 Adventure Class/22. RPG/22.00. RPG Genre\|RPG]] instead of a Rail Shooter. It's an unbelievably good game, but it saw a difficult development cycle and a release with little fanfare. By the time the next Panzer Dragoon came out with the original style, *Orta* (Smilebit, 2002), it was after the original team was disbanded and after Sega stopped manufacturing their own consoles.

Meanwhile, Star Fox had a similarly rocky story post-N64. The next release, *Assault* (2005), had its development outsourced to [[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.04. Namco\|Namco]] and itself featured a mix of some on-foot [[30 - 39 Social Class/33. TPS/33.00. TPS Genre\|TPS]] segments. *Star Fox Command* (Q-Games, 2007) on the DS incorporated strategy mechanics and then laid dormant as a series for nearly a decade after. When *Star Fox Zero* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.15. PlatinumGames\|PlatinumGames]], 2016) released, it was criticized for gimmicky controls. Over and over, it seemed like Star Fox was trying to break away from the subgenre but couldn't ever quite stick the landing.

What else was there? *Silent Iron* (Phoenix Games, 2003), and the very unusual *Killer7* (Grasshopper, 2005) which might not even be part of this subgenre.
- The new wave of downloadable digital titles was a boon for the [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/10. Arcade Class\|Arcade Class]] more generally, which Sega took advantage of with *After Burner Climax* (2006)

Really, there are only two other incredible mainstream standouts, both of which are contenders for the best in the subgenre (or some of the best games ever?)
- A long awaited Wii followup, Treasure's *Sin & Punishment: Star Successor* (2009)
- [[90 - 99 Resources/92. People/92.13. Masahiro Sakurai\|Masahiro Sakurai]] reimagining the long-dormant Kid Icarus franchise with *Kid Icarus: Uprising* (2012), which seemed like the perfect modernization of the subgenre while being tarred by the control limitations of a handheld console

Anyway... then it's all indies. *Ex-Zodiac* (MNKY, 2022), *Mirage Feathers* (Oyasumi Workshop, 2024), , and so on. But with a recent remake of Star Fox 64, who knows...?

## Super Subgenres

Don't think there are any major ones of note - though perhaps there could have been if any of the attempts to incorporate other genres were successful.

## Alternates

There is not much to find here. Even the impact of Perspective is limited to Isometric super-scaling or full 3D.

A small - very small - number of games exist with Modifiers:
- *Sayonara Wild Hearts* (Simogo, 2019) adds **Rhythm**.
- *Whisker Squadron: Survivor* (Flippfly LLC, 2024) and *Rogue Flight* (Truant Pixel, 2024) add **Roguelike**.
