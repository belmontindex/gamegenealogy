**Fixed Shooters** are those in which the player character moves along a single axis and fires shots to defeat waves of enemies.

<video src="https://dillonbelmont.com/02genealogy-assets/12/12.1/shooterfix.mp4" controls width="100%"></video>
*Space Invaders* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.02. Taito\|Taito]], 1979); *Galaga* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.04. Namco\|Namco]], 1981)



## Super-Subgenres

### Gallery Fixed Shooters
The **Gallery Fixed Shooter** is relatively rare, though quite a few entries released between 1988 - 1990. It takes cues from the [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/12. Shooting/12.8. Gallery Shooter\|Gallery Shooter]] subgenre, with enemies appearing anywhere on screen and a more flexible reticle. However, this is merged with the player controlling a hitbox restricted to movement along one axis.

<video src="https://dillonbelmont.com/02genealogy-assets/12/12.1/fixgallery.mp4" controls width="100%"></video>
*Cabal* (TAD Corporation, 1988); *Wild Guns* (Natsume, 1994)

## Alternates
The limitations of Fixed Shooters, as well as their short time in the limelight, means there are not many alternates to be found. The most common is the Isometric Fixed Shooter, which generally follows the same formula but with the screen slightly tilted:
<video src="https://dillonbelmont.com/02genealogy-assets/12/12.1/isofix.mp4" controls width="100%"></video>
*Radar Scope* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.06. Nintendo\|Nintendo]], 1980); *Juno First* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.05. Konami\|Konami]], 1983)

Beyond that, *Charge 'n Blast* (Sims, 2000) provided a 3D perspective:
<video src="https://dillonbelmont.com/02genealogy-assets/12/12.1/charge.mp4" controls width="100%"></video>

The only Fixed Shooter with a gameplay modifier I have found so far is [[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.05. Konami\|Konami's]] *Block Hole* (1989), which adds Tetris-like [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/15. Puzzle/15. Puzzle Genre\|Puzzle]] elements.
