**Tube Shooters** take the fundamentals of [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/12. Shooting/12.1. Fixed Shooter\|Fixed Shooters]] and wrap the movement around the screen to provide a pseudo-3D effect.

<video src="https://dillonbelmont.com/02genealogy-assets/12/12.3/shootertube.mp4" controls width="100%"></video>
*Gyruss* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.05. Konami\|Konami]], 1983); *iS: internal section* (Positron, 1999)

This is a pretty small subgenre. If you've heard of any Tube Shooter, it's probably *Tempest* ([[90 - 99 Resources/91. Companies/91.03. Atari\|Atari]], 1981). The subgenre does still serve as an interesting relic of history due to its attempt to take [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/12. Shooting/12.1. Fixed Shooter\|Fixed Shooters]] 3D before the advancements from [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/12. Shooting/12.5. V-SHMUP\|V-SHMUPs]] or [[10 - 19 Arcade Class/12. Shooting/12.9. Rail Shooter\|Rail Shooters]] came about.

These games are often associated with psychedelic or trance-like visuals. Perhaps this is Jeff Minter's lasting mark on the subgenre, as best seen in Llamasoft's *Tempest 2000* (1994) or *Polybius* (2017), but the gameplay does seem conducive to that aesthetic regardless. Some others worth checking out, both on the PS1, are *N2O: Nitrous Oxide* (Gremlin Interactive, 1998), and *iS: internal section* (see above).

Oh, and there is a game with a [[70 - 79 Modifiers/70. Modifiers\|Modifier]]! *Aaero* (Mad Fellows, 2017) brings a **Rhythm** component to the mix. Hooray!
